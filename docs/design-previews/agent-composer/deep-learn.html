Capturing photons: the role of pigments

Photosynthesis begins when a pigment absorbs light. This turns incoming solar energy into a signal that can be moved, stored, and eventually used to make sugar.

The site of action: the chloroplast

The light-dependent reactions occur across the thylakoid membrane. Pigments are held there in precise arrangements, so absorbed energy can be directed toward a reaction center instead of fading away as heat.

Key idea

Chlorophyll absorbs specific wavelengths of visible light. In a reaction center, captured energy starts an electron transfer.

From color to chemical work

Different pigments absorb different parts of the spectrum. Together, they broaden the light a plant can use and pass energy efficiently from one molecule to the next.

This arrangement matters because absorbing a photon is only the first move. A photosystem helps preserve the energy long enough to begin chemical work.
